# REAL-USER SESSION SCRIPT — FAMILY SHIELD WEEK 13
**Purpose:** Conduct five honest usability sessions with people who could realistically receive or act on an urgent family call. At least three of the five must be strangers to the researcher.

## Recruitment filter
Include people who have a family member they regularly coordinate with and who could plausibly be asked to send money in an urgent call. Do not count a person as a stranger if they are a close friend, relative, or teammate. Aim for a mix of parents aged 45–65, older adults who manage their own calls/payments, and adults who regularly support parents or coordinate family matters.

## Consent opener
“I am testing a student prototype called Family Shield. It does not detect fake voices, verify callers, contact anyone, or block payments. I want to observe whether the instructions are understandable. We will use a fictional scenario. Please do not share real names, phone numbers, family passwords, bank details, or private incidents. You can stop at any time. May I take anonymous notes and record your exact words without identifying you?”

Record consent: yes/no. If no, do not proceed or record their words.

## Neutral task prompt
“Imagine you receive a call that sounds like your adult child. The caller says there has been an accident and asks you to transfer money immediately. Please use this prototype and tell me what you would do next. Think aloud if you can.”

Do not teach the person how to use the prototype. Do not tell them where to click. If they ask for help, ask: “What would you try if I were not here?” Record the help needed.

## Observe, do not lead
- Do they understand “Pausa antes de pagar”?
- Do they know to call the family member using a number already saved, not the number that called them?
- Can they follow family member → trusted contact 1 → trusted contact 2?
- Do they understand that the app does not make calls or verify identity?
- Do they confuse “I checked independently” with “the app verified it”?
- Can they find and understand the pause fallback?
- Where do they hesitate, misread, backtrack, or abandon?

## Ranking the three worst pains
After all five sessions, group repeated issues. Rank the three most serious observed pains using severity 1–5, frequency (participants affected, 0–5), threat to 10:1 value (1–5), and fix feasibility (1–5). Explain the evidence and exact participant quotes behind each rank. Do not fill rankings before collecting the sessions.

## Fix, redeploy, retest
Fix the #1 noncritical usability pain. Do not fix a cosmetic issue if the observed problem could cause false confidence or a payment mistake. Redeploy to Vercel. Repeat the same fictional task with comparable users or conditions. Capture dated before/after observations.

## Return signal
A return signal is a voluntary second visit, an unsolicited message, or a signup. Do not count “I like it”, an answer to “would you use this?”, or a researcher-invited second task.
